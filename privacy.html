Privacy

Privacy policy

Leaf · Last updated 31 October 2026

Leaf does not collect, store or share your personal data. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no third-party tracking. The developer runs no server for Leaf, so nothing about you or your device is sent to the developer.

What Leaf keeps on your device

To work, Leaf keeps these in its private storage on your device: the list of sources you follow (with any notification choices), the stories you have saved, which page you are on, which stories you have read, a copy of recent stories so the app opens quickly offline, and your reading and notification settings. Deleting the app removes all of it. Your sources, saved stories, reading place and read marks can be part of your own device backup, which only you control. The copy of recent stories is left out of the backup.

iCloud

If you are signed in to iCloud, Leaf keeps your sources, saved stories, reading place and read marks in step between your devices using Apple's iCloud key-value storage. This data is stored in your own iCloud account under Apple's terms and privacy policy. The developer cannot see it. To stop syncing, turn off iCloud for Leaf in the Settings app, or sign out of iCloud.

Network

Leaf contacts the websites of the sources you follow to download their feeds and the pictures in them, and it contacts a website you type in to find its feed. Those websites can see your IP address and the usual details of a web request, including that it comes from Leaf, as with any browser or reader. They handle that under their own privacy policies. Leaf sends them no account, no identifier and no information about your other sources.

When you choose Read full story, the article opens in the system's built-in web view. The website sees you as it would in Safari. Leaf cannot see what you do there.

Leaf uses your device's country setting on the device itself to suggest sources. It is not sent anywhere.

Notifications and background refresh

Leaf asks for permission to send notifications only when you turn one on. Notifications are created on your device when iOS lets Leaf check your sources in the background. There is no push notification server and no device token is sent anywhere. You can turn notifications off in Leaf or in the Settings app.

Widget

The Leaf widget shows headlines that the app has already downloaded. They are shared between the app and the widget through storage on your device.

Subscriptions

Leaf Plus is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, and the developer does not receive your name, Apple ID, payment details or purchase history. Leaf checks on your device with Apple whether your subscription is active. The developer may see sales numbers in App Store Connect, which Apple provides in aggregated form.

Importing and exporting sources

If you import an OPML file, Leaf reads only the file you pick. If you export one, it is saved or shared where you choose through the system. Leaf does not send it anywhere.

Permissions

Leaf asks only for notifications, and only if you turn them on. It does not use your camera, microphone, location, contacts or photos.

Apple

If you install Leaf through TestFlight or the App Store, Apple may collect usage and crash information under Apple's own privacy policy. The developer sees only what Apple chooses to share, such as tester feedback you send through TestFlight.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to access, correct or delete on the developer's side. You control everything Leaf stores by removing sources, clearing saved stories, turning off iCloud for Leaf, or deleting the app.

Children

Leaf does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with an updated date.

Questions

Open an issue on the project's GitHub page and I will answer there.
